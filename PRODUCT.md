# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Designers and potential employers evaluating Craig's design expertise. They arrive with a critical eye — they can read a layout, they notice when something is off, and they form a professional judgement fast. They're looking for evidence of experience and rigour across UI and UX for both apps and web.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio and writing platform that demonstrates Craig's depth as a designer. Success is a visitor who leaves feeling confident in Craig's capabilities and either reaches out, bookmarks the site, or follows future work. The site itself is a demonstration of craft — it should show the work clearly and get out of the way.

## Brand Personality

Minimal, precise, confident. The site earns trust through restraint, not spectacle.

## Anti-references

- Portfolios that bury the work under transitions, parallax, or hero animations
- Sites with excessive color treatments, gradient overload, or accent-color noise
- Trendy aesthetics with no underlying design thinking (glassmorphism, 3D blobs, aurora backgrounds)
- Anything that distracts from reading the work — busy backgrounds, floating elements, decorative clutter
- Generic "creative agency" templates: big rotating hero text, grid of identical hover-reveal cards, footer with 6 social links

## Design Principles

1. **The site serves the work.** Every element steps back. Navigation, layout, and typography exist to deliver the portfolio, not to compete with it.
2. **Precision over decoration.** If an element can't justify its presence, remove it. Spacing, alignment, and proportion do the visual work.
3. **Confidence through restraint.** Strong typographic hierarchy and committed whitespace communicate authority without explanation.
4. **Swiss discipline.** Grid-anchored layouts, rational type scales, colour used sparingly and with intent. Norgram and Fictive Kin are the tonal references.
5. **Substance is the spectacle.** The design thinking in the work is what should impress. The site's job is to make that thinking visible and legible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. High contrast ratios (the near-black/white palette already supports this). No motion required for comprehension — avoid animations that convey meaning unavailable through static state.
